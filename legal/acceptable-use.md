# Acceptable Use Policy

Effective 2 October 2026

Crowdfill makes it easy to create a lot of responses. That is useful when you are building, testing or demonstrating your own forms, and harmful when it is pointed at someone else's. This policy is part of our [Terms of Use](terms.html).

## Good uses

- Testing your own forms before you share them
- Load-testing a form, and the spreadsheets, scripts and dashboards connected to it
- Producing demo and sample data for presentations, teaching and product screenshots
- Showing how results look when respondents lean positive, negative or split
- Checking that validation rules, branching and required questions work the way you expect

## Not allowed

- Submitting responses to forms you don't own or don't have permission to test
- Manipulating surveys, polls, votes, elections, contests, giveaways, petitions, reviews or research run by others
- Flooding or disrupting a form, or trying to make it unusable for real respondents
- Getting around limits a form owner has set, such as sign-in requirements or "one response per person"
- Entering real people's personal data without their consent, or impersonating someone
- Submitting content that is illegal, hateful, harassing or meant to deceive
- Anything that breaks Google's terms of service or the law where you are

## What happens if it's misused

We may stop supporting, or report, misuse we become aware of. Form owners and Google may block responses, and misuse can have legal consequences for the person doing it.

## Reporting

If you believe Crowdfill is being used against your form, write to [support@dyrt.io](mailto:support@dyrt.io). Google Forms also lets you limit responses to signed-in users, or to one response per person, in the form's settings.
